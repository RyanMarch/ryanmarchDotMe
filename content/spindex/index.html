The Product

Spindex turns digital record collections into an interactive 3D crate in the browser.

Spindex is a browser-based companion for vinyl record collectors. It connects directly with Discogs accounts to turn flat catalog lists into a virtual record crate. Collectors can flip through album sleeves, inspect jacket artwork, pull out discs to view color vinyl pressings, read liner notes and lyrics, and run a dedicated album art screensaver right beside their turntable.

Physical record collecting is an inherently tactile hobby. Enthusiasts spend hours flipping through wooden crates, admiring gatefold photography, and checking record runout etchings. Yet digital collection tools usually present collections as static rows. Spindex restores the physical feeling of crate digging to digital collections.

It features no advertising, requires no Spindex account, and comes with a pre-packaged 12-album demo crate so anyone can start exploring immediately.

The 3D record crate: interactive sleeve physics paired with dynamic ambient lighting that matches the active album cover.

Key Features

Digging the Crate: Records sit stacked, ready for you to scroll through and explore. The ambient background lighting dynamically adapts in real time to the dominant color palette of the album in focus. Three view modes accommodate different browsing styles:

Stack View: large artwork with album title and artist name.
Grid View: cover gallery for rapid scanning across albums.
List View: structured catalog table showing details like duration, genre, and release dates.

Gatefold Album Inspector: Pulling any record from the crate opens up the liner notes for the album. View realistic virtual vinyl discs peeking from jackets, including standard black vinyl, translucent pressings, marble swirls, splatters, and picture discs. Flip jackets to inspect back cover artwork, slide out song lyrics, read about album concept and production, and view endless details about each pressing.

Finding & Filtering: Search your entire collection instantly across titles, artists, and songs. Jump to a letter or decade, or filter by disc diameter, playback speed, and format. When listeners need fresh inspiration, the Surprise Me shuffle pulls a random album.

Live Barcode Scanner: Add new records to collections straight from a smartphone or webcam.

  • Low-Light Utility: Built-in flashlight toggle and camera switcher for dimly lit rooms.

Turntable Screensaver & Collection Insights: Designed for display next to an audio setup or mirrored to a large screen, Screensaver Mode fills displays with a floating album collage that periodically highlights records from the collection.

Product Strategy

Discogs is the de facto catalog for vinyl collectors, but it is built for record-keeping, not for the joy of browsing. As the Product Manager and creator of Spindex, I set out to close that gap. Make a collection feel as good to flip through as the real thing, without asking collectors to give up their data, create another account, and share in the joy of music.

I owned the product from concept through UX prototyping, serverless architecture, and launch. Several record collectors I worked with wanted a product that could augment their collection and their music experience without attempting to replace the act of listening to their physical media.

Five decisions shaped the result:

  • Bridging Physical and Digital Experiences: Identified that vinyl collectors value both tangible rituals and administrative efficiency. Designed the product around physical metaphors like crate flipping, gatefold inspection, and vinyl color matching.
  • Frictionless Discovery with Demo Crates: Provided an instant, fully populated 12-album demo crate accessible on first load. Visitors experience the full feature set, 3D physics, and lyric sheets before committing to a Discogs connection.
  • Privacy-First Architecture: Spindex has no third-party analytics or tracking scripts, and only uses anonymized Cloudflare Web Analytics for high-level visitor counts. There is no user database: collection data lives in local browser storage, and Discogs sessions use HttpOnly cookies. That protects user privacy and means there is no stored user data to secure or leak.
  • On-Demand Synchronization: Avoided aggressive background pollers and scheduled cron jobs that drain mobile batteries. Sync operations run on-demand with incremental checks.
  • Listening Room Optimization: Tailored viewing modes for specific collector contexts: barcode scanning on mobile, high-density grid browsing on desktop, and ambient screensaver walls on tablet stands beside turntables.

Technical Architecture

Spindex is engineered using modern vanilla web technologies, pairing browser capabilities with lightweight serverless edge functions:

  • Vanilla Web Stack: Built entirely with native ES modules, semantic HTML5, and CSS custom properties.
  • Serverless Cloudflare Pages Functions: Lightweight edge endpoints handle Discogs OAuth 1.0a authentication, relay external APIs (Wikipedia, MusicBrainz, LRCLIB), and snapshot storage.
  • Zero Secrets in the Client: Discogs API consumer secrets and user access tokens are kept off the client. Sessions are encrypted into secure HttpOnly cookies using AES-GCM-256 encryption.
  • Edge Caching & Image Relay: An edge-cached image relay proxies album artwork through Cloudflare edge caching, minimizing latency and eliminating external CORS obstacles.
  • Service Worker & Offline PWA: A network-first Service Worker caches application assets. Once synchronized, collectors can browse artwork, tracklists, and pressing details completely offline.
