The Product

Speakeasy is a craft cocktail companion and recipe library for the modern home bartender.


Speakeasy is a web app and recipe library made to sit on your kitchen counter or bar cart. It pairs classic and modern cocktail recipes with interactive glassware visuals, inventory tracking for your home bar, and practical tools for mixing drinks.

There are no subscription screens, paywalls, or walls of ads before showing you an ingredient list. Speakeasy takes the opposite approach: browse hundreds of curated drinks, check off the bottles you own, and find out what you can mix right now. Then, start adding your own custom recipes and planning out your next round of home bartending.

The home screen: curated shelves, drink collections, and live indicators showing what you can make.


How It Started

Over the years, I've developed a genuine love for making cocktails at home. I've enjoyed reading bartending books like Death & Co and the Savoy Cocktail Book, hunting down obscure amari, and learning how small tweaks in ratios change a drink.

The problem was the tools. I kept jumping between recipe notes on my phone, bookmarked websites cluttered with lifestyle essays, and recipe apps with clunky interfaces. I needed a place to keep my favorite recipes in addition to "standard" recipes. And I wanted to see what I could make with what I already had on hand.

I wanted a tool that reflected the craft itself: clean, thoughtfully made, easy to read across the counter, and practical when your hands are covered in citrus juice. It should be easy and engaging to browse yet also quick and useful when you're ready to start prepping.

Key Features

Dynamic Glassware Visualization: Every cocktail renders inside the glass it belongs in: coupes, rocks glasses, highballs, Nick & Noras, and even tiki mugs. Ingredients stack as proportional color layers based on their measurements. If you hover over an ingredient in the recipe, its layer lights up in the glass. For shaken or stirred drinks, a quick toggle switches to a blended view simulating the final cocktail color.

The counter view shows ingredients as layers in glassware, easy-to-read instructions, and flavor profiles.

Backbar Inventory & Shopping Math: Check off the spirits, liqueurs, fortified wines, and bitters on your shelf. Speakeasy uses an ingredient taxonomy that understands relationships. If you own bourbon, it counts toward drinks that call for whiskey. Common brand names and alternate spellings are included in the search as well.

The shopping list figures out which single bottle purchase unlocks the highest number of new drinks, so you can build out your bar cart with a clear plan.

Quick-Paste Recipe Creator: Adding a custom cocktail is as simple as copying text from a website or book and pasting it in. The parser reads amounts, fractions, and units, and then guesses the right glass, method, and garnish.

Details That Matter

I wanted Speakeasy to feel like a book you might find in a vintage cocktail bar while also providing the utility of an interactive app. Several small touches make a difference here:

  • Inline Timers: When a recipe says to stir for 30 seconds or shake for 15, a button appears in the instructions. Tapping it starts a countdown timer to help you achieve proper dilution.
  • Screen Wake Lock: Keeps your screen lit while prepping and pouring so you aren't repeatedly unlocking your phone with wet or sticky fingers.
  • Unit Toggle: Switch between ounces and milliliters and all ingredient amounts and dilution calculations adapt on the fly.
  • Recipe Riffs: Easily make your own riffs or browse variations of classic cocktail recipes with nods back to their roots. Like the Boulevardier evolved from the Negroni, showing the relation between drinks.
  • Substitution Suggestions: If you're missing an ingredient, Speakeasy suggests a similar substitute based on what you have on hand.
  • History and Context: Cocktail recipes have short writeups describing origin, history, and flavor profiles.
  • ABV Percentage: Estimate a drink's percentage of alcohol by volume, accounting for dilution.
  • Calorie Estimation: Estimate a drink's calorie count based on ingredients and dilution.
  • Servings Adjustment: Adjust recipe to make more or fewer servings while keeping proportions the same.
  • Sharing: Share a link to a classic or custom recipe so friends can make it at home.
  • Help Center: User guides in the help center provide more information about using Speakeasy.

Passwordless Login

Speakeasy works offline and stores everything in your browser by default. You can use it as a guest indefinitely without signing up.

When you want your bar inventory and custom recipes to sync across your phone, laptop, or tablet, Speakeasy uses a passwordless login code sent straight to your inbox:

Enter an email to receive a six-digit code.
Your sign-in code will arrive in your inbox.

Under the hood, email dispatch is separated from the main web application:

  • One-Time Codes: Each code is random, single-use, and expires after 10 minutes.
  • A Separate Mail Service: Sending that code is handled by a small service that lives outside the main app, instead of being bolted onto it. If that mail service ever fails, it can't take the actual cocktail app down with it.
  • Automatic Data Merge: Once you enter the code, your local drinks and bottle list automatically merge with your cloud account, making everything available on any device.

The Vault

Signed-in accounts get a settings hub for everything behind the scenes: which bar is active, how the app should look and measure, and a running tally of what you've actually poured. You'll even see your rank increase from novice to enthusiast, to head mixologist and beyond based on how many ingredients you have stocked, how many custom cocktails you add, and how many drinks you've made.

Switch between multiple bars (say, a home bar and an office kit), and see your pour count and go-to drink at a glance.

Hitting I Made This on any recipe logs it to your history, so you can remember what you've made.

Your data is portable. Export your full bar and recipe library as a single backup file whenever you want, and import it on a new device. Nothing about your bar is locked inside Speakeasy's servers.

My Role

As the product manager and creator, I shaped Speakeasy from early problem definition through interface design, architecture, and launch.

  • Problem Definition: Studied how people look up drinks at home. Most people rely on memory, bookmarked websites, or generic apps. I focused the product around visual clarity, bottle readiness, and zero barriers to entry.
  • Local-First Choice: Chose a client-first model where all data lives locally in the browser by default. This makes the app fast, reliable offline, and free from account setup friction.
  • Smart Inventory Logic: Designed the "Bottle-Next" recommendation algorithm so home bartenders can make deliberate choices about what to buy rather than picking up bottles at random.
  • Lightweight Stack: The app is built with plain HTML, modern CSS, and vanilla JavaScript. Without heavy framework overhead, the app loads fast and runs smoothly on phones and tablets alike.

Takeaways

Building Speakeasy brought together two things I care about: thoughtful product design and craft cocktails. Making something useful for the bar cart meant focusing on speed, clean typography, visual feedback, and practical utilities that fit the physical space.

By keeping the barrier to entry low, making inventory useful, and prioritizing clean client-side performance, Speakeasy gives home bartenders a reliable companion they can open every time they step behind the counter.
