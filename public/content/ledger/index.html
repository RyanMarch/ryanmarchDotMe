Ledger

Private, local-first personal finance tracker for one person or a couple.

Product Manager & Creator • 2026

The Product

The Together view combines both partners' shared finances, counting joint accounts once.

Ledger is a personal finance tracker that pulls in balances and transactions automatically from connected accounts and lets two people see their finances together. It brings net worth, monthly spending pace, budgets, investments, and retirement planning into one place without ads, upsells, or recurring subscription fees.

Your financial records stay in a private encrypted vault inside your browser rather than sitting in a company database. Every account, transaction, and backup is locked on your device before it is saved, so no third party can read your balances or sell your spending habits.

Ledger is self-hosted for personal use, so I also put together a live demo loaded with sample accounts for two partners. You can click through the Personal, Partner, and Together views right in your browser.

How It Started

For years I relied on Mint and Personal Capital to keep track of spending, balances, and investments. Both were eventually bought and functionality deteriorated. Mint shut down, and Personal Capital turned into a wall of wealth management pitches and upsells.

When I started looking for a replacement, I thought I might be able to solve a problem for my partner and me. I wanted to see our net worth, investments, and spending together in one place without having to repeatedly update a shared spreadsheet by hand. None of the apps I tried automated household finances cleanly for two people.

I built Ledger so two people can see their personal financial picture and a combined picture without merging every single account. App-based interactivity mean that it can provide a better experience than any spreadsheet ever could by pulling in real-time transactions and balances from both people automatically.

Seeing Finances Together

Two people can share a single Ledger setup without giving up separate accounts or private logins. Each person keeps their own locked vault with their own passphrase, then decides what to share with their partner on an account-by-account basis:

  • Per-Account Visibility: Mark any account as Private (only you can see it), Shared (visible to your partner), or Joint (linked by both of you).
  • No Double-Counting: When both partners connect the same joint checking account, house, or mortgage, Ledger pairs them so balances and transactions count once in the combined view.
  • Synced Categories: When either person categorizes a transaction on a joint account by hand, the change carries over to the other partner's view. Automatic bank rules never overwrite a manual choice.
  • Switch Views Anywhere: Toggle between Personal, Partner, and Together at the top of the page to view combined net worth, shared monthly budgets, upcoming bills, and a joint retirement plan.

Key Features

Full Financial Picture & Automatic Bank Sync: Track cash savings, brokerage accounts, retirement funds, real estate, credit cards, and loans together in one place. Connect accounts directly through Plaid for automatic daily updates, import CSVs or historical spreadsheets, or keep manual accounts current with configurable scheduled update reminders. Ledger also pulls automated home value estimates and other properties or assets so you can see your total net worth.

The main Overview page tracks net worth over time alongside monthly spending pace and cash flow.

Spending Pace, Budgets, Searchable Register: Ledger plots your day-by-day cumulative spending against the previous month so you know right away if you are running ahead or behind your usual rhythm. Set flexible category budgets, use custom count rules so a bill clearing on the 31st always counts toward the next month, track upcoming card due dates, and check the blended effective APY earned across your interest-bearing accounts.

The Transactions tab includes weekly spending breakdowns and instant search with the / shortcut, supporting filters like >100, cat:groceries, and OR queries.

Investment Tracking: View holdings, cost basis, gains since purchase, and asset allocation across brokerage and retirement accounts. Target allocation checks flag which asset classes are underweight so you know where to direct new contributions. Investment buys and dividend reinvestments stay isolated from your everyday register so they never skew monthly spending totals.

Retirement Planner: Run a month-by-month simulation across cash, taxable brokerage, Roth IRA, and 401(k) accounts to find the earliest date you can stop contributing while keeping your money lasting through your target end age. The planner pulls real spending from your transactions, models bridge years before retirement accounts unlock, factors in Social Security ages and mortgage payoff dates, and includes a 401(k) paycheck front-loading calculator. And of course it works for one person or two people together.

Phone Companion & Weekly Digest: Check balances and spending on the go with a lightweight mobile view that saves right to your phone's Home Screen. On macOS, you can also add Ledger to your Dock from Safari so it opens in its own standalone window.

  • Quick Biometric Unlock: Open the mobile view with Touch ID, Face ID, or a passphrase.
  • Essential Snapshot: Shows net worth history, current balances, monthly budget progress, recent transactions, and retirement projections without keeping bank tokens on your phone.
  • Bank Alerts: Optional push notifications alert you if a bank connection disconnects and needs attention, without ever putting dollar amounts in a notification.
  • Configurable Weekly Digest: A scheduled weekly summary covering 7-day net worth changes, top account movers, spending pace, and manual accounts due for an update.

Five Different Themes (Each with Light & Dark): Evolv is the default theme shown above, along with four other built-in themes: Foundation, Color Pop, Metal, and Workstation. Every theme supports both light and dark modes and follows your system preference automatically unless you override it.

Product Decisions

Most couples don't fit an all-or-nothing approach to money. Some accounts are joint, some are shared so both people can see the full picture, and some stay individual. Designing Ledger around per-account sharing and paired joint accounts reflects how households actually work day to day.

On the spending side, I chose to focus on monthly pace instead of strict envelope budgeting. Comparing where your spending sits today against the same point last month tells you quickly if a month is running hot, without making you categorize every coffee on the spot.

Small details matter when you look at your finances every week. Everyday transactions show cents while long-term retirement projections round to whole dollars. In Together mode, if a partner hasn't shared their spending or retirement age, the planner flags what's missing instead of quietly printing an overly optimistic date.

Unlike some of my other projects, there is no separate help center site for Ledger. Both the user guide and full manual are built right into the app under Settings > About, so everything stays in one place.

Technical Details

A quick look at how Ledger is built:

  • Vanilla Web Stack: Built with plain HTML, modern CSS custom properties, and native ES modules with no frontend framework or build step. Core financial calculations (accounting, budgets, mortgage amortization, retirement simulation, and partner merging) live in pure, unit-tested modules separated from the UI.
  • Client-Side Encryption: Vault data, bank access tokens, balances, and transaction history are encrypted in the browser via WebCrypto (PBKDF2 and AES-GCM) before being written to IndexedDB. Biometric unlock uses WebAuthn PRF so Touch ID and Face ID can unlock the local vault without storing plaintext keys.
  • Self-Hosted on Cloudflare: While your primary vault lives in your browser, a small set of Cloudflare Pages Functions handles Plaid syncing and stores encrypted blobs in Workers KV for partner sharing, mobile snapshots, and optional encrypted backups. The server never sees plaintext financial data. Cloudflare Access gates sign-in for both users, and a nightly cron Worker refreshes balances and sends disconnect alerts and weekly digests.
  • Demo Site: Because the real app sits behind private access controls, I also launched a demo site pre-loaded with sample data for two partners to show off the feature set.

The repository is private while I maintain Ledger for personal use, though I'm happy to share it if you're interested in self-hosting a copy. Feel free to reach out if you'd like to take a look.
